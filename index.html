        How the projection works

        Each number is the expected full-PPR score from a ridge regression trained on every regular-season game since 2020, refit each week using only games played before it.

        • Usage and form: recent points, targets, carries, target and carry share, air yards, snap share, and red-zone, goal-line and end-zone opportunities.
        • Matchup: points the defense allows to the position versus league average, plus the Vegas implied team total and spread.
        • Availability: the player’s own injury status and practice participation, and opportunity left by teammates who are ruled out or on IR.
        • Role changes: when a starter is ruled out or on IR, his usual carries and targets are re-shared among the players who are left, so the next man up is projected for starter volume.
        • Rookies and new players: after two NFL games they are projected from their own usage like anyone else. Before that they get a typical projection for their position and situation and are tagged “new”.
        • Weather: a small adjustment for wind over 15 mph or temperatures below 32°F or above 85°F outdoors.

        The range bar shows the 10th to 90th percentile of what players with similar projections actually scored in the backtest.

        Why ridge regression

        Seven model types were backtested week by week over 2025 and 2026 Weeks 1–9 with no look-ahead. The linear models and the neural network tied for the most accurate; ridge was chosen because it is stable, repeatable and its weights are explainable.

        Scored on fantasy-relevant players (top 30 RB, 40 WR, 15 TE each week). Start/sit = how often the model picked the higher scorer from a same-position pair.

        Track record: this model vs ESPN

        Each week both sets of projections are frozen before kickoff, then scored on the same players once the games are final. Players who did not play are left out of both.
